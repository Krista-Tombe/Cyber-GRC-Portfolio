## 1. Policy Statement
The purpose of this policy is to establish a baseline for the information security requirements for a company to ensure the protection of its information assets, 
technology systems, business operations, and stakeholder data from unauthorized access, disclosure, alteration, destruction, and disruption. This policy will include managing information 
security risks, assigning accountability, implementing appropriate safeguards, monitoring compliance, and responding to security incidents, with the intention of 
meeting applicable legal, regulatory, contractual, and business requirements.

## 2. Scope
This Information Security policy applies to all company staff, as well as all entities that may use or are granted access to internal business information. This
additionally includes, but is not limited to, contractors, temporary employees such as interns, third party service providers granted system access, or company volunteers. 

## 3. Policy Objectives   
1. Establish clear accountability for information security and risk management.
2. Identify information assets and associated risks.
3. Apply access controls and safeguards to information sensitivity.
4. Detect, report, investigate, and respond to suspected security incidents.
5. Maintain appropriate backup, recovery, and business continuity capabilities.
6. Assess third-party security risks before and during relevant business relationships.
7. Evaluate compliance with approved policies and remediate identified deficiencies.
8. Maintain documentation and evidence demonstrating the operation of security controls.

# 4. Governance and Responsibilities 
This section will clearly outline the roles and responsibilities in maintaining and enforcing the Information Security Policy within the company. 

## 4.1 Management
The board of management will have the final say in the execution of Information Security policy guidelines and decisions. Executive management will approve the top-level security policy, allocate financial and personnel resources, and reviews security policy semi-annually to ensure that it still meets the company guidelines. 

## 4.2 Head Information Security Officer
The head of the Information Security officer is the lead on designing, upkeeping, and educating the company faculty on the Information Security Policy. Additionally, the Head of Information Security will be required to perform regular gap analysis, risk assessments, and briefing the management on the results, and updating the policy accordingly. 

## 4.3 IT Team
The IT team is responsible for implementing and maintaining the technical safeguards established by the approved policy. They must maintain asset inventories, system configurations, access records, and any other relevant operational evidence. Additionally they must escalate security incidents, control failures, and material risks to the head of information security and appropriate management.

## 4.4 Workforce Members 
Company employees are required to abide by the set policies in place to ensure workplace data security. Security awareness trainings must be completed regularly, and employees are expected to report suspected phishing, unauthorized access, data exposure, lost devices, and other security incidents as soon as they occur. 

## 4.5 Third Parties
Third parties with access to organizational information or systems shall comply with applicable contractual security requirements and approved access restrictions. The responsible business owner shall ensure that relevant security obligations are established before access or data sharing begins.

# 5. Information Asset Management


## 5.1 Asset Inventory
The organization will be required to build and maintain a central catalog of all hardware, software, databases, applications, documents, and data flows. As part of this inventory, it is imperative that the Asset "Owner" must be be assigned to the inventory when setup to establish accountability for security decisions. 

## 5.2 Information Classification
Organizations typically sort information into tiers based on risk, confidentiality, and impact: 

• Public: Information with no negative impact if disclosed  \
• Internal-Only: Data meant strictly for employees and authorized business partners  \
• Confidential: Sensitive records where disclosure causes significant harm or regulatory issues  \
• Restricted / High Risk: Maximum-security data requiring strict access controls and encryption  

Information owners shall determine appropriate classifications and handling requirements. Where classification is unknown, employees are expected to protect the information at an assumed higher sensitivity level until its classification is established.

# 6. Identity and Access Management

The organization will restrict access to information and systems according to business need, least privilege, and separation of duties when it is appropriate.
Minimum requirements include:

Each workforce member must use an individually assigned account for routine access.  \ 
Multi-factor authentication (MFA) should be required for access to systems containing Confidential or Restricted information. This may include MFA to access the worksite physically, accessing computer systems, or any company information above the public confidentiality level.
Privileged access should be limited to authorized personnel and separate from routine user access where technically feasible. \
Access rights will be reviewed at least quarterly for privileged accounts and at least semiannually for other in-scope accounts.  \
Access must be modified or revoked promptly following role changes, termination, contract expiration, or changes in business need. Terminated users' access will be disabled promptly at the coordinated time with Human Resources. \
Authentication secrets will not be shared or stored in unapproved locations. Suspected credential compromise must be reported immediately.  \
Authentication and authorization mechanisms should be configured according to approved standards and applicable risk requirements.

# 7. Acceptable Use Policy

Organizational systems, accounts, devices, and information is only to be used for authorized business activities and in accordance with applicable law and contractual obligations. 

Users will not:  \
Attempt unauthorized access, privilege escalation, or security-control circumvention.  \
Install unapproved software or connect unauthorized devices where prohibited by organizational standards.  \
Disable security tools or monitoring mechanisms without authorization.  \
Transfer Confidential or Restricted information to personal accounts, unapproved storage, or unauthorized applications.  \
Use organizational resources to distribute malicious software, conduct unlawful activities, or compromise other systems.  \
Submit sensitive organizational information to artificial intelligence tools or other external services unless the service and use case have been specifically approved. 

The company must communicate any applicable monitoring practices used and privacy expectations in accordance with law and organizational procedures.

# 8. Network Security
The company must maintain proper security configurations to ensure minimal risk to essential company systems. 

Minimum network maintenance include:
Supported operating systems and applications must be maintained and updated regularly.  \
Security patches will be prioritized according to severity, exploitability, and business impact.  \
Critical vulnerabilities are assessed promptly and remediated within an established risk-based remediation period. Exploited or actively threatened vulnerabilities shall receive expedited treatment.  \
Network security controls are to be deployed where appropriate to the environment and risk.  \
Firewalls and network access controls must restrict unnecessary inbound and outbound communications.  \
Secure configuration baselines will be documented for system standardization and reviewed following significant changes.  \
Unauthorized software, services, and exposed interfaces must be identified, categorized and addressed appropriately.  \
Unsupported or end-of-life technology will be upgraded, isolated, or covered by an approved risk exception and compensating safeguards.

# 9. Data Privacy 
The organizations will protect information throughout its lifecycle, including collection, use, storage, transmission, and disposal.

Minimum privacy requirements include:
Information access will be limited to authorized individuals that have been determined to have a business need.  
Confidential and Restricted information will be transmitted through approved channels using appropriate encryption safeguards.  \
Sensitive information that must be stored on portable devices and/or removable media must be encrypted when able.  \
Data collection, processing, sharing, and retention will be limited to authorized business purposes.  \
Information must not be disclosed externally without appropriate authorization and contractual or legal review when required. \
Data retention and secure disposal must follow approved retention schedules based on data location.  \
Suspected unauthorized disclosure, loss, or alteration of sensitive information must be reported through the incident response process within the designated timeframe.

# 10. Logging, Monitoring, and Detection
The organization must maintain an up to date log and perform regular monitoring appropriate to system criticality and risk.

At a minimum:
Security-relevant events are to be logged for critical systems and services where supported.  
Logs shall be protected against unauthorized access, modification, and deletion.  \
System clocks shall be synchronized where practicable to support event correlation and investigation.  \
Alerts and suspicious activity shall be reviewed according to documented monitoring procedures.  \
Log retention shall be established based on business needs, risk, legal requirements, and investigative needs.  \
Monitoring coverage and alert handling shall be periodically assessed for effectiveness.  \
Log record details must cover relevant authentication events: \
 -privilege activities  \
 -access failures  \
 -security alerts  \
 -significant configuration changes.

# 11. Security Awareness and Training
All workforce members will be required to complete security awareness training during onboarding, and will be required to take follow up trainings annually afterwards.

Training should address the following as applicable:
-Phishing, spear phishing, and social engineering tactics.  \
-Credential protection and MFA.  \
-Data classification and secure handling.  \
-Incident identification and reporting procedure.  \
-Acceptable use and remote-work security if applicable.  \
-Privacy obligations and secure use of approved technology.  

Personnel with privileged or specialized security responsibilities must receive role-appropriate training. Training completion and policy acknowledgments will be recorded and retained as compliance evidence.

# 12. Incident Response 
All suspected information security incidents must be reported promptly through the company designated reporting channels.

The organization must maintain documented incident response procedures covering:
Preparation: Define roles, reporting channels, escalation criteria, and response resources.
Identification and analysis: Assess the credibility, scope, severity, and potential business impact of suspected incidents.
Containment: Limit further harm through authorized actions.
Eradication and recovery: Remove identified threats, restore affected services, and validate system integrity.
Notification and escalation: Notify management, legal counsel, affected stakeholders, regulators, or contractual partners when required.
Post-incident review: Document root causes, lessons learned, and corrective actions.

Incident records should include relevant information such as: 
-timelines
-decisions
-actions
-evidence
-assigned follow-up activities. 

Evidence will be handled in a manner appropriate to its potential investigative or legal significance. Suspected incidents involving restricted information, privileged account compromise, ransomware, or material service disruption shall be escalated immediately to the designated incident response authority.

# 13. Backup / Recovery Plan
Critical systems must be included in documented backup and recovery arrangements based on business impact and risk.

Identification of critical systems, data, and dependencies must be recorded based on business risk. 
Defined recovery time objectives (RTOs) and recovery point objectives (RPOs) for critical services must be clearly defined.  \
A plan for protection of backups against unauthorized access, alteration, and deletion must be established in place.  \
There should be separation or isolation of backup copies where appropriate to reduce the impact of ransomware and other destructive events.  \
Periodic testing of backup restoration and recovery procedures should be done regularly.  \
Documented business continuity and disaster recovery responsibilities.  \
If material deficiencies identified during recovery tests, there must be a remediation log formed of changes made. 

Recovery testing should occur at least annually for critical services, and more frequently where business or contractual obligations require it.

# 14. Third Party Risk Management
Third parties that access organizational information, provide critical technology services, or process sensitive data will be expected to maintain a standard level of security that will ensure the protection of the company.

Before onboarding a material third party, the responsible business owner and relevant security or procurement personnel will assess applicable risks, which may include:
Information sensitivity and access privileges.
Security policies and relevant control practices.
Independent assurance reports or other appropriate security evidence.
Incident notification and cooperation obligations.
Data handling, retention, and secure disposal commitments.
Business continuity and service availability.
Subcontractor dependencies and material changes in service delivery.
Contracts shall include appropriate security and privacy requirements, incident notification provisions, and other protections based on the service and applicable obligations.

Third-party risk will be reassessed periodically and when significant changes, incidents, or material concerns arise.

# 15. Handling Risk
The organization must maintain a documented process for identifying, assessing, prioritizing, handling, and monitoring information security risks.

Risk assessments shall consider:
Threats and vulnerabilities.
Likelihood and potential business impact.
Confidentiality, integrity, and availability implications.
Existing safeguards and their effectiveness.
Legal, regulatory, contractual, and reputational consequences.
Dependencies on critical systems and third parties.

Identified risks will be logged in a risk register with an assigned owner, risk rating, treatment decision, planned actions, target dates, and current status.
Risk treatment options may include mitigation, avoidance, transfer, or acceptance, as appropriate.

Residual risks exceeding established risk tolerance will be escalated to the designated approval authority. Risk acceptance must be documented and will not override mandatory legal, regulatory, or contractual obligations.

# 16. Vulnerability Management
Material systems may be subject to vulnerability identification, remediation tracking, and controlled change management.

Changes to production systems, security configurations, access controls, and critical services will:
Have a documented business justification.
Receive appropriate authorization before implementation, except for approved emergency procedures.
Include risk and impact assessment proportionate to the change.
Include testing and rollback or recovery planning where appropriate.
Be documented with implementation and validation evidence.

Emergency changes will be documented and reviewed after implementation as soon as practicable. Vulnerability remediation deadlines and exceptions will be tracked to closure or formally approved risk treatment.

# 17. Policy Review
The policy owner shall review this policy at least annually and following significant events, including:
Material changes to business operations or technology.  \
Significant security incidents or emerging threats.  \
Changes to applicable legal, regulatory, or contractual requirements.  \
Material audit findings or control deficiencies.  \
Changes to the organization's risk tolerance or security program.  \
Revisions shall be documented, reviewed by appropriate stakeholders, and approved by the designated approval authority before becoming effective.  \
Superseded versions shall be retained according to the organization's document retention requirements.
